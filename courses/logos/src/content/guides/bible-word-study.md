## Start a word study

The fastest way is from a Bible:

1. Right-click the word.
2. In the left pane, choose the **lemma**, the dictionary form of the word.
3. On the right, click **Bible Word Study**.

Or select the word and click **Study** in the Selection Menu. You can also start from **Guides / Workflows**.

To study the word in part of the Bible only, click **All Passages** and type a passage or range.

## What your Bible needs

A word study works from a Greek or Hebrew Bible whose words are **tagged** (each word marked with its dictionary form and grammar). It also works from an English Bible with a **reverse interlinear**: an English Bible linked word by word to the Greek or Hebrew.

## English word or original word?

The **Prefer Lemmas** setting in Program Settings decides what **Study** reports on:

- **Yes:** the Greek or Hebrew word under the English word.
- **No:** the English word itself.

## Studying a Greek or Hebrew word

The main sections include:

- **Translation:** a ring chart of how your Bible translates the word. Change the Bible under the section's **Settings**.
- **Septuagint Translation:** for a Hebrew word, how the Greek Old Testament translates it.
- **Root:** related words. Click one to start a new word study.
- **Senses:** the meanings of the word, from the Bible Sense Lexicon.
- **Example Uses**, **Phrases** and **Lemma in Passage**.

## Studying an English word

The sections include **Hebrew Words** and **Greek Words**, shown as a ring chart: the original words that this English word translates. **Textual Searches** uses your five highest-priority Bibles.

## For translators

The translation ring answers a common question: how have translators rendered this word, and how often each way? Compare it with the senses before you choose a word in your own language.

What you see depends on your Logos plan. Some sections may be missing.
