## How Logos labels books

Every book in Logos has a **type**. You can filter the Library by type (see [Your library](~/guides/library/)). Some labels are not what you might expect:

- Most Bible dictionaries and encyclopedias are labelled **Encyclopedia**.
- The UBS Handbooks, SIL Exegetical Summaries and Semantic and Structural Analyses are labelled **Bible Commentary**.
- Critical apparatuses have their own type, **Bible Apparatus**.

The lists below give a few well-known titles of each kind. **In TW** means the title is in SIL's Translator's Workplace contents list of June 2025. Your own library may be different.

## Bibles

The original-language texts, early translations and modern versions. Translators work from the original text and compare versions.

| Title | In TW |
|---|---|
| The Greek New Testament, 5th edition (UBS5), with its apparatus | Yes |
| Biblia Hebraica Stuttgartensia (BHS), with its apparatus | Yes |
| Septuaginta (Rahlfs), the Greek Old Testament | Yes |
| Nestle-Aland 28th edition (NA28) | No |
| Biblia Hebraica Quinta (BHQ) | No |

An **interlinear** shows the Greek or Hebrew with its English words. A **reverse interlinear** is an English Bible with the Greek or Hebrew word lined up under each English word. TW also has SIL's Translator's Reference Translations of some New Testament books.

## Lexicons

A lexicon is a dictionary of Hebrew, Aramaic or Greek words. It lists each word's possible meanings, and sometimes where they occur. A **semantic domain** lexicon groups words by meaning, which helps when you look for a word in your own language.

| Title | In TW |
|---|---|
| Brown-Driver-Briggs Hebrew and English Lexicon (BDB) | Yes |
| Louw and Nida, Greek-English Lexicon of the New Testament Based on Semantic Domains | Yes |
| UBS Concise Greek-English Dictionary of the New Testament | Yes |
| HALOT (Hebrew and Aramaic Lexicon of the Old Testament) | No |
| BDAG (Greek-English Lexicon of the New Testament) | No |

Logos also has its own **Bible Sense Lexicon**. It is a tool, not a book, and is part of every subscription. It sorts the senses of Hebrew and Greek words, and the Bible Word Study uses it.

### Translator's Notes: Notes (TNN) and Display (TND)

The Translator's Notes are written for translators who speak English as a second language. Each book comes in two parts, and in Paratext each part is its own resource:

- **TNN, the Notes.** They help you understand the message of the whole book, how its parts fit together and what the text means in detail. They point out the parts that are hard to translate, and give other translators' ideas for how to say the meaning. They also explain customs, grammar, figures of speech and how the discourse is built.
- **TND, the Display.** Each verse, or part of a verse, is shown first as a **source line** in bold, taken from the NIV. Where the NIV does not follow the interpretation the Notes recommend, a second source line comes from a version that does. Then come one or more **meaning lines**, which say the same meaning in plain words. Verse parts are numbered the same way in the Display and the Notes (1:5a, 1:5b).

The two work together: the Notes *explain* the meaning, and the Display *says it again* in plain lines. The Display itself warns you not to translate any of its lines word for word. Think how to say the meaning in your own language.

In Translator's Workplace the two parts are also separate resources, for example "Translator's Notes on Proverbs: Notes".
