Greek often uses short words to join ideas. They are easy to skip, but they decide how two parts of a sentence relate: does the second part give a **reason**, the **content** of what someone thought, a **place**, a **time**? Translations sometimes differ only because they read one of these small words differently.

Greek words here are written in English letters, with a gloss.

## *hoti*: "that" or "because"

*Hoti* has two common uses:

- **Content ("that").** It introduces what someone said, thought, knew or hoped. Luke 24:21: "but we had hoped **that** he was the one who was going to redeem Israel" (NIV).
- **Reason ("because", "for").** It gives the reason for what was just said.

Usually the context makes it clear. Sometimes both fit. In **Luke 1:45**, Elizabeth says Mary is blessed, and then *hoti* introduces the fulfilment of what the Lord told her. Is Mary blessed because she believed **that** it would happen (content)? Or is she blessed for believing, **because** it will happen (reason)? Versions differ.

A translator has to choose, because most languages must mark the difference.

## *en*: "in", "at", "when"

The preposition *en* most often means "in". But with a word for time or for an event, it can mean "at" or "when".

In **Matthew 2:2** the Magi say they saw his star *en tē anatolē*, "in the rising". *Anatolē* can mean the east (where the sun rises) or the rising itself. So the phrase can mean "in the east" (where they were when they saw it) or "when it rose" (the moment they saw it). The NIV reads it the second way.

## What to look for when checking

- **Mark the small words** in the Greek of the passage, using an interlinear if needed.
- **Compare versions** at each one. Where they differ, there is a choice.
- **Ask how the draft links the two parts.** A back translation often shows it plainly: "and", "because", "that", "when".
- **Check the helps.** Translator's Notes and the UBS Handbooks usually discuss these words where they matter.
- **Check the choice fits the context**, using the method in [Weighing interpretations](../weighing-interpretations/).

A wrong connector does not change any key term, so it is easy to miss. But it can change the logic of a whole verse.
