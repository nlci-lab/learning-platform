## The six steps at this level

1. **Find the problem.** Who has made the Father known: “Son” (υἱός) or “God” (θεός), with or without the article ὁ (“the”)?
2. **List the readings** yourself from the manuscripts, as in the card above.
3. **External evidence.** Take the six questions in order; number comes last. “God” has the oldest Greek witnesses (𝔓⁶⁶ and 𝔓⁷⁵, about 200–225; א and B, 4th century). “Son” is spread much more widely. The external evidence leans a little to “God”, but not clearly.
4. **Internal evidence.** The UBS committee (in Metzger's *Textual Commentary*) argues that “Son” is the easier reading, and that scribes made 1:18 match John 3:16 and John 3:18. Bart Ehrman argues the other way: a scribe who wanted to stress Christ's deity had a reason to change “Son” to “God”. Each side can explain how the other arose.
5. **Weigh it up.** The UBS committee printed “only God” with a \{B\} rating, by a majority. A careful team can reach either reading.
6. **Decide.** Write a recommendation for your Review Committee: the reading you propose, the main reasons, and a footnote giving the other reading.

The full case, with a table for each step, is in the worked example [John 1:18: only God or only Son?](~/guide/examples/john-1-18/).

## Decide with your team

At every level, the decision is made with your Review Committee, following your text policy. At John 1:18 the worked example recommends following the UBS text, worded so that “God” is not heard as a second God, with “the only Son” in a footnote. If the committee chooses “Son” to match the regional-language Bible, that is a sound choice too, with “God” in the footnote. See the [decision tree](~/learn/decisions/#dtn-textual-decision--decide) and [Which text should a translation follow?](~/guide/text-policy/).
