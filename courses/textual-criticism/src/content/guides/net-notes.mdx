## What you need

In Paratext, open the **NET08** resource (NET Bible, New English Translation). Its notes are marked by type. The textual notes are marked **tc** (“Textual Criticism”). Other notes are translator's notes (tn) and study notes (sn).

We do not show the note itself here: the NET Bible's permission for free use covers its Bible text, not its notes. Open it in your own Paratext and read it beside this page.

## John 1:18 in the NET note

In our own words, the NET textual note on John 1:18:

- calls the problem “notoriously difficult”: the choice is between **μονογενὴς θεός**, “the only God”, and **ὁ μονογενὴς υἱός**, “the only son”;
- points out that in the manuscripts the two words differed by only one letter, because both were written in short form (*nomina sacra*);
- groups the many readings into two families: those with θεός (“God”) and those with υἱός (“Son”);
- says most manuscripts, especially later ones, read “Son”, while the oldest papyri and major early codices read “God”, and judges that the external evidence strongly supports “God”;
- adds that “God” is the harder reading, and that it explains how “Son” arose: scribes would easily change “God” to the familiar “only Son” of John 3:16 and 3:18 and 1 John 4:9;
- answers an objection from Bart Ehrman about the grammar, and explains the NET's own wording.

The NET translates: “The only one, himself God, …”.

## The six steps at this level

1. **Find the problem.** The note names it.
2. **List the readings.** The note groups the readings and names the main witnesses for each.
3. **External evidence.** Given, with the manuscripts named.
4. **Internal evidence.** Given: which reading is harder, and which explains the other.
5. **Weigh it up.** The note weighs both kinds of evidence and gives its verdict.
6. **Decide.** You now see the arguments, but the weighing is still the NET team's.

## Read a note critically

- A note argues for the reading its Bible chose. Ask what the other side would say.
- Check the witnesses it names against the UBS apparatus (Part 4). Notes shorten lists.
- Notes use the same terms as the apparatus: *nomina sacra*, “harder reading”, the signs for manuscripts. The [glossary](~/glossary/) explains them.

## What this level can tell you

| Can tell you | Cannot tell you |
|---|---|
| The main witnesses for each reading | The full list of witnesses |
| The arguments on both sides | How the UBS committee weighed them |
| One team's reasoned verdict | Whether another team would agree |
