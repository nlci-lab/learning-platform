NA28 dates Irenaeus to the 2nd century, but his works are known mainly from later translations. Trobisch points out that NA28 dates the Armenian translation (Irᵃʳᵐ) to the 4th and 5th centuries. He adds that this translation may still keep a 2nd-century form of a New Testament text, because it was made from Irenaeus's own writings.

So when you see Irenaeusˡᵃᵗ in the apparatus, ask two questions. What did Irenaeus write? And did the translator, or a later copyist, change the quotation?
